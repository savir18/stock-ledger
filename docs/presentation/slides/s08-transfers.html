Transactions in practice

Transfers and corrections

Transfer: one transaction, two rows

  • TRANSFER_OUT (−n) at the source, TRANSFER_IN (+n) at the destination
  • Linked by one transfer_id: both rows or neither
  • Source stock is checked under lock, so it never goes negative
  • Warehouse rows are locked in ascending id order: opposite transfers cannot deadlock

Correction: a new row, not an edit

  • A REVERSAL row has the exact opposite quantity
  • It points to the original through reverses_ledger_id
  • The original is never touched and shows as reversed
  • An entry can be reversed once; a reversal cannot be reversed

Slide 8 (about 60 seconds). Two applications of the same transaction pattern. A transfer is the classic atomic operation: debit one warehouse and credit another, as two ledger rows tied by a transfer_id. Mention the deadlock-avoidance rule: always lock the lower warehouse id first. A correction is just another ledger row. The trigger enforces the rules, so even a direct SQL call cannot reverse the same entry twice. Live demo: Record and transfer page, move stock between warehouses, then Ledger history and click Reverse; the original row is struck through and a REVERSAL row appears.
